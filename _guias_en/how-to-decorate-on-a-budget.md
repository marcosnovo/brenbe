Decorating on a budget isn't decorating "cheap": it's **deciding well** where each dollar goes. The most common mistake is spending on what shows right away (objects, art, pretty things bought one at a time) and running out of money for what really changes how a room is lived in. This guide gives you an order of priorities, criteria for saving without it showing and a method for working in stages. It doesn't include prices, because they depend on timing, location and each purchase: use the criteria and apply them to your own numbers.

## Before you spend: three things that cost nothing

1. **Declutter and clear.** Take out everything you don't use, and donate or sell what's left over. A clear room looks more cared for with zero investment.
2. **Rearrange.** Turning the sofa, moving the bed, shifting the bookcase: the layout can change a room more than a new piece of furniture.
3. **Look at what you already have.** A chair that can be painted, a crate that becomes a nightstand, a print that can be framed differently.

Then measure the room and write down what bothers you most. That decides where to start.

## Where to invest and where to save

| Invest in... | Save on... |
|---|---|
| Mattress, bed base, the sofa you actually sit on | Decorative objects, art, vases |
| A good lamp and quality bulbs | Simple storage furniture frames |
| The piece you use daily (the table, the desk chair) | Pillows, throws, small plants |
| Curtains with good drape (if you need them) | Frames and shelves |
| A well-sized rug | Coffee table and side tables (secondhand or homemade) |

The logic is simple: **invest in what you use every day and lasts for years; save on what's easy to swap out.**

## Priorities: what to buy first

1. **Lighting.** A floor or table lamp with a warm bulb changes a whole room. It's the best return in all of decorating.
2. **Large textiles.** A well-proportioned rug, curtains longer and wider than the window, a decent coverlet.
3. **Storage.** Whatever takes clutter out of sight: a low cabinet, matching bins, shelves.
4. **The main piece in the room.** The sofa in a living room, the bed in a bedroom, the table in a dining area.
5. **Color.** Paint on one wall or on a piece of furniture, if your lease and your mood allow it.
6. **Objects, art and plants.** Last, once you know what the room is missing.

## Secondhand furniture: how to buy well

Secondhand furniture is one of the best ways to decorate on a budget, and it has more character than mass-produced pieces. You can find it on **online marketplaces and local buy-and-sell groups**, at **thrift stores, flea markets and antique malls**, at garage sales and estate sales, at furniture consignment shops and among people you know.

What to look at:

- **Structure and solid wood.** More important than the look: a well-made wood piece can be sanded, painted or fitted with new hardware.
- **Measurements.** Bring a tape measure and your floor plan; check that it fits through doors and stairwells.
- **Upholstery and mattresses.** Check the smell, the stains and that there are no pests before you buy; if in doubt, pass.
- **Drawers and doors.** They should open and close properly.
- **Transport.** Count on a truck, a delivery service or a friend's help: sometimes it's part of the cost.
- **Negotiation.** It's common; ask if there's room on the price.

One warning: don't buy a piece "because it's cheap" if you hadn't planned on it. A piece you don't need, even a cheap one, is money badly spent.

## Do it yourself: refresh before you buy

- **Painting a piece of furniture** (with the right prep and paint for the material) can give it a second life.
- **Swapping knobs and pulls** is among the cheapest changes and it shows.
- **Framing** prints or photos in matching frames organizes a wall.
- **Re-covering or swapping the covers** on pillows and chairs, or reupholstering a simple seat.
- **Making a headboard** from a piece of fabric and a wooden rail, or from a shelf.

Be realistic: doing it yourself saves money, but it costs time and tools; if a project frustrates you, it's better to buy the solution. And if you rent, ask before painting or changing anything that isn't yours.

## Work in stages

Instead of trying to solve the whole home at once, make a plan:

1. **Stage 1:** declutter, lighting and textiles for the room you use most.
2. **Stage 2:** the main piece (sofa, bed, table), chosen without rushing.
3. **Stage 3:** storage and color.
4. **Stage 4:** objects and details.

Set a cap for each stage and stick to it. That way you avoid impulse purchases and every step improves something concrete. ## Mistakes that get expensive

- **Buying without measuring.**
- **Buying a complete furniture set** instead of pieces chosen one at a time.
- **Spending on decor before lighting.**
- **Changing styles halfway** and ending up with purchases that don't go together.
- **Paying twice for cheap:** buying something poorly made that breaks and then replacing it.
- **Ignoring the extra costs:** delivery, assembly, hardware, paint, custom window treatments.
