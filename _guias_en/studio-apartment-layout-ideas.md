A studio of 270 to 375 square feet (25 to 35 m²) can feel spacious or cramped with exactly the same furniture: what changes is **how you arrange it**. Before choosing a style or buying anything, it's worth solving a floor plan: where each activity goes, how much room it needs and how you move through the space. This guide gives you a method, reference measurements and three typical layouts to start from. If you're after more general decorating ideas, start with our guide to [studio apartment decorating ideas]({{ '/en/guides/studio-apartment-decorating-ideas/' | relative_url }}); here we focus on layout.

## Step 1: draw a floor plan with real measurements

Measure the walls, the door and window openings, the **swing of every door** and the location of outlets, radiators and the air conditioner. Draw it on graph paper or in a spreadsheet; a simple scale is enough (one square for every 4 inches, about 10 cm). Mark these too:

- Where natural light comes in and at what time of day.
- Where the fixed features are (kitchen, bathroom, plumbing).
- Which furniture you already own and want to keep, with its dimensions.

Without this plan it's very easy to buy a sofa that "fits" but blocks the window, or a table that cuts off the way to the bathroom.

## Step 2: define the zones and their order

A studio fits, at minimum, four zones: **sleeping, lounging, cooking/eating and storage**. Sometimes a fifth is added: working. Zones are best ordered **from public to private**:

1. **Entry and storage.** A spot for keys, coats and shoes prevents clutter from the very first step.
2. **Kitchen and dining.** Near the plumbing, with a table that can be small or foldable.
3. **Living area.** In the middle or by the brightest window.
4. **Sleeping.** As far as possible from the entrance and the kitchen, for noise and privacy.
5. **Work.** If it's daily, by the window and with its own light; if occasional, make it something you can put away.

## Step 3: the measurements worth respecting

These are common ergonomic distances. They're guidelines: adjust them to your space and your body.

| What | Reference measurement |
|---|---|
| Main walkway (from the entrance to the zones) | 32 to 36 in (80 to 90 cm) clear |
| Secondary walkway (between furniture) | 24 in (60 cm) minimum |
| Beside the bed (at least one side) | 20 to 24 in (50 to 60 cm) clear |
| At the foot of the bed, walkway | 24 to 28 in (60 to 70 cm) |
| Sofa to coffee table | 14 to 18 in (35 to 45 cm) |
| Front of the sofa to the wall or other furniture | 36 in (90 cm) or more if people walk between them |
| Dining chair: room to pull it out | 28 to 36 in (70 to 90 cm) behind the chair |
| Work table depth | 22 to 28 in (55 to 70 cm) |
| Dining table height | about 30 in (75 cm) |
| Kitchen: aisle between counters | 36 to 42 in (90 to 110 cm) |

A practical rule: if you have to "dodge" a piece of furniture to get by every day, it's in the wrong place, no matter how nice it is.

## Three layouts to start from

These are starting points; every real floor plan has its own quirks (windows, door, bathroom).

### Layout 1: the long rectangle (270 to 325 sq ft, or 25 to 30 m², window on the short wall)

- The **bed** at the far end, by the window, with the headboard against the side wall.
- In the middle, the **living area**: a sofa with its back to the bed, which works as a divider, and a rug that marks the zone.
- Near the entrance, the **kitchen and a bar or folding table**, plus storage.
- Advantage: the sleeping area is visually set apart. Watch out: the living area can turn into a hallway; keep a clear path of 32 inches (80 cm) along one side.

### Layout 2: the square (325 to 375 sq ft, or 30 to 35 m², window on a long wall)

- **Living area by the window** (the most-used zone during the day).
- **Bed in the opposite corner**, with a low bookshelf or open shelving as a boundary.
- **Dining table** against a wall, with a bench or two chairs, that opens up when you have guests.
- Advantage: light reaches both the living area and the desk. Watch out: the bed shouldn't end up in the path to the bathroom.

### Layout 3: the one with a balcony

- If the balcony connects to the room, use it as an **extension**: a desk or a reading nook against the glass, or the dining table.
- Keep the doors and the way out clear; don't use it to store boxes.
- Advantage: you gain a useful zone without adding square footage. Watch out: use changes between winter and summer; plan the area with curtains and good lighting.

## Multifunction furniture: what's worth it and what isn't

Furniture that does two jobs is very useful in 270 to 375 square feet, if you pick it for **real use** and not just to save inches on a plan.

- **Bed with storage underneath or drawers:** among the most worthwhile; it frees up a whole closet.
- **Drop-leaf or extendable table:** a table 28 inches (70 cm) on a side that stretches to seat four.
- **Storage bench** at the entry or at the foot of the bed.
- **Wall-mounted fold-down desk** or a narrow console table that doubles as a desk.
- **Low bookshelf** that divides the space and stores things at the same time.
- **Sofa bed:** only if nighttime use is occasional.

Avoid pieces that "unfold" and force you to move other furniture: in the long run you stop using them.

## Storage and lighting: two things that organize a layout

**Storage** (closets, drawers, high shelves) needs to be planned on the floor plan, not solved afterward with boxes in plain sight. Use the height: shelves up to the ceiling on one wall, off-season clothes in drawers under the bed.

**Lighting** zones a room too: a floor lamp in the living area, a reading light by the bed and a direct light over the work table. When the light changes, the room reads as if it were divided. ## Common mistakes

- **Pushing everything against the walls** and leaving an empty middle that nobody uses.
- **A sofa that's too big**, which blocks the way or the window.
- **A bed in plain view from the front door**, with no visual boundary at all.
- **Not leaving room to open** doors, drawers, closets and the fridge.
- **Many small pieces instead of a few well-chosen ones**, which looks cluttered.
- **Not planning storage.**

## When the floor plan doesn't work

If you've tried three layouts and none works, there's probably a constraint (a column, a badly placed window) that an outside eye can solve.
